The DewLab Curriculum

What we teach, how a lesson works, and how twelve lessons are shared across six learning worlds.

Six guides. Six worlds. One mission: grow, learn and thrive together.

Who it’s for

Elementary-aged children, and the grown-ups beside them.

01

Kindergarten through 6th grade

A wide span, so every lesson carries a grade band rather than pretending one version fits a five-year-old and an eleven-year-old equally. The same topic can appear twice, pitched differently.

02

A child can do most of it alone

Read-aloud on every prompt, pictures over paragraphs, and one obvious next step at all times. The grown-up is needed for the account, the scissors and the stove — not for the learning.

What a lesson is

Every lesson in every world moves through the same five stages, so children always know where they are.

1
Before-checkFour minutes, picture-led, read aloud. No grades, no wrong answers shown — it marks the starting line.
2
Meet us liveA real guide, roughly forty-five minutes. Somewhere in the middle they say a secret word.
3
Try it at homeOne hands-on project. Materials listed, steps one at a time, and a note on why it works.
4
Play & practiseShort tap-and-play activities. Repeatable, forgiving, and impossible to fail.
5
Show what you grewThe same skills checked again. Different questions, so it measures learning rather than memory.

Safety runs through all five

Every lesson carries its own safety line, because the risk changes with the world — a hot stove in Nourish, tools in Create, and in Preserve the acidity and processing times that make food safe to eat. Those numbers are never drafted or adapted. They are taken from the National Center for Home Food Preservation exactly as published, or the lesson does not ship.

Roughly a week per lesson. Fifteen minutes on screen before the session, forty-five live, then an hour or so of hands and play across the days after. Screen time is the smallest part on purpose.

What makes it different

Three things, and every lesson has to hold all three.

👤

A real person, live

Not a recorded course. A guide in the actual garden, answering the question a child just asked.

🌱

Hands in the soil

Every lesson ends up off the screen. A seed in a cup, a worm in a jar, a jar of something you made.

📈

Learning that can be shown

Before and after, skill by skill, mapped to state standards. Most programmes report attendance. This reports what changed.

What DewLab isn’t

Worth writing down, so the scope holds.

Deliberately out of scope

  • Not homework. Nothing is assigned, graded or due. A child who does none of it has still lost nothing.
  • Not a screen-time replacement. The screen is the smallest part of every lesson by design.
  • Not self-paced-alone. The live session is the spine. Without it, the rest doesn’t unlock.
  • Not a curriculum library. A small number of finished lessons, not a searchable archive of hundreds.
  • Not a school platform. No classes, no rosters, no teacher accounts — families, not institutions.
